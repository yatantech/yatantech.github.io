Short Fuse privacy policy

Last updated: 17 November 2026

Short Fuse is a puzzle game made by Yatan Tech ("we", "us"), an independent developer. This page explains what information the game uses, why, and the choices you have. If anything is unclear, write to yatantech@gmail.com.

The short version

  • There are no accounts, no sign-in and no purchases.
  • Your progress and settings are saved only on your phone.
  • In the European Union the game shows no ads and sends no analytics.
  • In the UK, Switzerland, Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein we ask you first. Nothing starts until you answer, and you can change your answer at any time.
  • Everywhere else the game shows ads, which pay for it, and collects anonymous play statistics to make the levels better.

Information saved on your phone

The game saves your level progress, coins, boosters, a level you were in the middle of, settings (sound, vibration, reminders) and your privacy choices in a file on your phone. It is not sent to us. Uninstalling the game deletes it.

To decide which privacy rules apply, the game reads the country of your app store account (the App Store storefront on iPhone, or your Google Play account country on Android). This happens on your phone, the result is kept on your phone, and we do not send it anywhere. We do not use your location or your IP address for this. If the country cannot be read, the game treats you as being in the European Union.

Play statistics (analytics)

Outside the EU (and in the countries above, only if you choose "Allow"), the game uses Unity Gaming Services Analytics to understand how levels are played, so we can make them fairer and more fun. It records game events such as starting, winning or losing a level, the level number, the number of pieces turned, the time taken, why a level was lost, and when a booster, coins, a retry, a rewind or a rewarded ad is used. Unity also records basic technical details such as device model, operating system, app version and an approximate country, together with a random identifier for this installation. These statistics are not linked to your name or contact details.

Ads

Outside the EU, the game shows ads through Unity LevelPlay and the ad networks it works with (currently Unity Ads). All ads are optional rewarded videos that you choose to watch, for example to rewind the fire, for a retry, a free booster or extra coins. To show ads, measure them and prevent fraud, these services may collect device information, your IP address, and, if you allow it, your device's advertising identifier.

  • iPhone: the game asks for permission through Apple's "Allow tracking" prompt. If you choose not to allow it, you still see ads, but they are not personalised using your advertising identifier.
  • Android: you can reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's settings.
  • UK, Switzerland, Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein: if you choose "Don't allow", ads are not personalised and no analytics are collected.

These services act under their own privacy policies. See Unity's Game Player and App User Privacy Policy, which covers Unity Ads, LevelPlay and Unity Analytics.

Reminders

If you turn on reminders, the game schedules a notification on your phone to tell you when your free retries or your daily reward are ready. These are created by the game on your phone. No message is sent from a server and no contact details are needed. You can turn reminders off in your phone's notification settings.

Your choices

  • Change your privacy answer: tap the pause button (top right), then "Privacy". Where we ask for consent, this opens your privacy choices.
  • Tracking on iPhone: Settings, Privacy & Security, Tracking.
  • Delete your game data: uninstall the game.
  • Questions or requests about your data: email yatantech@gmail.com. Because the game has no accounts, we cannot look you up by name, but we will help as far as we can, including passing deletion requests on to Unity.

Children

Short Fuse is made for adults and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has used the game and you want data removed, contact us.

Changes

If we change how the game uses information, we will update this page and the date at the top.
